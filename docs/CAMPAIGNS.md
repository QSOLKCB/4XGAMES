# Campaign briefings

The original **Open Frontier** and **Machine Dawn** packs remain byte-for-byte
unchanged. Their expansion, scientific, conquest and council victories retain
legacy rules. Machine Dawn keeps its installed turn-eight fabrication event;
importing it as a text pack intentionally does not execute that JavaScript hook.

| Campaign | Geography | Playable empires | Objective pacing / deadline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grand Frontier: The Long Concord | 64 stars, 128 worlds, open grid | Meridian, Viridian, Basalt | Objective victory from turn 100; deadline 220 |
| Fallen Gates: The Quiet Meridian | 36 stars, 72 worlds, broken corridors | Lanterns, Glass, Hinterland | Objective victory from turn 70; deadline 180 |
| Siege of the Outer Rim | 40 stars, 80 worlds, two narrow interior corridors | Rimward Freeholds | Reconquest from turn 60; endurance turn 90; deadline 160 |
| Helix Accord: The Threefold Charter | 24 stars, 48 worlds, three interwoven arms | Lattice, Tidebound, Cinder | Exchange from turn 36; federation from 44; deadline 120 |
| Emberwake Exodus: The Last Convoy | 32 stars, 64 worlds, migration corridors | Ashwake, Gloam, Sunspindle | Convoy from turn 48; havens from 55; deadline 140 |
| Glass Meridian: The Unwritten Sky | 48 stars, 96 worlds, double transit ring | Prism, Mnemonic, Null Survey | Unwritten from turn 58; preservation from 65; deadline 170 |

These are turn targets, not estimates of real-world session length. Early military
elimination can still end an expedition. Relaxed, standard and veteran change AI
war timing; scripted event schedules are unchanged. They do not guarantee a
strict ordering of outcomes for every strategy/seed. All campaigns use the same
legal command rules and content limits.

## Grand Frontier: The Long Concord

Three expeditions reach the same unclaimed network. Meridian sees a commonwealth,
Viridian a living accord, and Basalt a durable order. Broad starlanes permit many
expansion choices; exposed borders make a compact early economy valuable.

Survey twelve stars and develop six colonies. Then choose:

- **Concord:** sign at least one trade pact, research Industrial Systems VI and
  keep a viable empire to turn 100.
- **Dominion:** hold eighteen colony worlds, research Directed Energy VI and
  reach turn 100.

Rivals pursue the same objectives. If multiple empires finish on the same turn,
score resolves the council decision. Trade avoids an expensive early war; a
territorial route needs shipyards, survey escorts and transport capacity. The
higher research costs make laboratories and observatories useful well beyond
first settlement. Supply grants and a council dispatch mark later phases.

Opening route: survey with the scout, settle nearby rich worlds, queue yards and
replacement arks, then build laboratories. Research prerequisites mean advanced
weapons need Industrial Systems III. For Concord, make contact and sign trade
before war starts; for Dominion, expand along several fronts and refit escorts.
The deadline is a defeat for the selected empire if it has not already won.

## Fallen Gates: The Quiet Meridian

The old transit network is silent. Horizontal corridors connect through a few
crossings; archives reveal fragments of a civilisation that chose to dismantle
its own roads. Every empire can interpret the fragments differently.

Survey eight stars, then recover two archives with survey-equipped ships. Choose:

- **Restore:** reach Industrial Systems V and Field Harmonics V; survive to
  turn 70 to stabilise the old network.
- **Path:** chart thirty stars and reach Warp Dynamics VI; survive to turn 70
  to establish independent routes.

Archive ownership is permanent, so scouting is a race. Build another survey ship
if the first is lost. Map crossings and faster drives matter more here than in
Grand Frontier. The archive reward helps fund the chosen programme; it does not
replace the research prerequisites. Route choice, recovered archives, completed
stages and rewards persist through saves. The deadline at turn 180 is a defeat
for the selected empire if another route to victory has not finished.

## Siege of the Outer Rim

The Freeholds begin with three worlds. Each occupying power holds five. Their
command systems sit at the far end of two corridors. The Directorate declares
war at turn ten; the Mandate follows at eighteen. Relief shipments arrive every
fourteen turns. Raiding groups begin at turns 25 and 45 and recur more frequently
as the siege develops, within the normal fleet cap.

Maintain four colony worlds to establish your foothold. Choose:

- **Endure:** research Directed Energy IV, then survive to turn 90.
- **Reclaim:** capture a world in the Directorate command system (31), then a
  world in the Mandate command system (39), and reach turn 60.

Reclaim needs marine ships and enough concentrated attack to overcome late
planetary defences. Use fleet groups to keep escorts together. Endurance rewards
energy reserves, defence grids and repair yards along the approaches. The relief
convoys help replace losses, but do not supply energy upkeep. Missing the turn-160
deadline is defeat. Completed liberation milestones remain recorded if a rival
later counterattacks; losing every colony and ark still ends the expedition.

## Horizons: nine cultures, three new expeditions

The three Horizons campaigns add **nine playable factions and 36 original hull
silhouettes**. The complete catalogue now contains eight campaigns and eighteen
campaign/empire choices. Each new campaign has three playable cultures, two
exclusive objective routes, an authored map, its own score and conditional
supply events. The existing five campaign directories are unchanged.

Horizons objectives belong to the selected player. Rivals expand, research,
trade, attack and can eliminate the player, but do not race an identical objective
charter. This makes the shorter new expeditions a different challenge from the
shared objective race in Grand Frontier. A missed deadline is a defeat. Completing
a prerequisite records a persistent milestone; later losing a colony or treaty
does not erase that completed stage. A surviving empire is still required.

### Helix Accord: The Threefold Charter

Three spiral arms meet through two sets of crossings. The **Lattice Compact**
starts with a small working federation; the **Tidebound Exchange** must turn its
larger reserve into colonies; the **Cinder Directorate** begins with heavier
hardware and stored research. Early contact can secure a trading partner before
later border pressure.

Survey eight systems and hold four colony worlds, then choose:

- **Exchange:** sign a trade pact to complete the open-ledger milestone, reach
  Industrial Systems III and survive to turn 36.
- **Federation:** reach Industrial Systems IV, hold eight colony worlds and
  survive to turn 44.

The exchange route earns recurring energy dividends after the trade milestone.
The federation route receives a one-time mineral grant after its industry
milestone. Exchange relays favour energy/research; fabrication cooperatives add
minerals and production. The deadline is turn 120. A faction's second world in
its home system is often a useful first settlement; scouts must travel along the
arms to reveal the crossings.

### Emberwake Exodus: The Last Convoy

The **Ashwake Flotilla**, **Gloam Gardeners** and **Sunspindle Union** inherit a
fractured chain of migration corridors. Horizontal routes cross only through
two vertical passages. Decide where replacement arks and escorts should be
built before sending every ship toward the next opening.

Survey ten systems and hold five colony worlds, then choose:

- **Havens:** hold nine colony worlds, reach Industrial Systems IV and survive
  to turn 55.
- **Convoy:** reach Warp Dynamics IV, chart twenty-four systems, develop Field
  Harmonics III and survive to turn 48.

Supply trains deliver minerals every twelve turns once the initial chart is
complete. Each route also has a conditional one-time supply reserve. Seed
sanctuaries support research; convoy depots combine production, energy and a
little planetary defence. These facilities supplement the ordinary shipyard:
the `yard` facility still enables ship construction and repairs. The deadline
is turn 140, and ordinary rival war timing eventually makes the corridors
contested space.

### Glass Meridian: The Unwritten Sky

The **Prism Cartographers**, **Mnemonic Choir** and **Null Survey** begin on
two concentric rings, joined by four spokes. A short-looking line on the chart
is not necessarily a route: movement follows starlanes. Archive discoveries
belong to the first survey-equipped arrival, so keeping a scout alive matters.

Survey twelve systems and recover two archives, then choose:

- **Preserve:** reach Industrial Systems V and Field Harmonics IV, then survive
  to turn 65 to contain the memory lattice.
- **Unwritten:** reach Warp Dynamics V, chart thirty-four systems and survive
  to turn 58 to establish independent routes.

The two-archive milestone unlocks a science grant. Archive vaults provide focused
research and defence; phase stations supply energy, science and construction.
Warp Dynamics V requires Industrial Systems III, and Field Harmonics IV needs
Warp Dynamics II. The deadline is turn 170. The outer ring offers a long sweep;
the four spokes shorten travel between distant sectors.

### New equipment and authoring compatibility

Every Horizons pack includes three additional equipment choices: a compact
two-lane drive at Propulsion III, a three-slot compression lance at Weapons IV,
and a bulky resonant shell at Shields III. They use the existing slot, cost and
research rules, so the designer can compare them against ordinary equipment.
No new component role or script execution is introduced.

The new maps, faction colours, hulls, starting resources, music, objective chains,
events and facilities live in the same editable `.txt` formats as the original
campaigns. All three packs fit the existing bounded Mod SDK exchange and also
support native pack, save and 3D Workbench layout roundtrips. Changes made in an
exported pack are imported as inert data.

## Reproducing and editing the campaigns

`node scripts/generate-campaigns.mjs` rebuilds all three packs through the same
pure authoring functions used by the workshop, including export/reimport before
writing. Original fiction, maps and objective tables are in that script; the
resulting `.txt` files are the runtime content. You can edit the runtime files
directly without running the generator. Rerunning it replaces those local edits.

`node scripts/generate-horizons.mjs` separately rebuilds **only** Helix Accord,
Emberwake and Glass Meridian. It uses the same validated native authoring and
export/import boundary, and never writes the existing five campaign directories.
Like the earlier generator, running it replaces edits in its own three target
directories; save a copy of authored changes before regenerating them.

`node --test tests/horizons.test.mjs` checks generation, the 36 distinct hull
shapes, native/SDK/Workbench map exchanges, and legal completion of both routes
for all nine new factions at seed 0. `node scripts/horizons-sweep.mjs` adds seed 7
and independent deterministic replays, validating every turn and every save
roundtrip. Use `SEEDS=0,7 DIFFICULTIES=relaxed,standard,veteran` for the bounded
108-scenario matrix; failures and losses are retained in the generated receipt.
These are automated policies, not claims that every human strategy wins.

`node scripts/campaign-sweep.mjs` drives the selected empire with a legal-command
AI policy, explicitly chooses each alternate route, validates every turn and
compares an independent replay. Use `SEEDS=2401 DIFFICULTIES=standard` for a shorter
run. Results distinguish campaign termination from a player win. Executed results
and limitations belong in [VALIDATION.md](VALIDATION.md); a hypothetical route or
an edited winning state is not a completed playthrough.
